Ten walls between you and the sin

Imam Ibn al-Qayyim listed ten reasons that give a servant patience against sin. This page turns each one into a wall you can build: his words in Arabic, the evidence, and a short drill you can do the moment the pull comes. Everything you write stays on your device.

Feeling the pull right now?

  1. Put the phone down or leave the room. Change your place first.
  2. Say aʿūdhu billāhi mina sh-shayṭāni r-rajīm.
  3. Pick one wall below and do its drill. Three honest minutes is enough to break the moment.

First: the three kinds of sabr

«الصبر ثلاثة أقسام: إمَّا صبرٌ عن المعصية فلا يرتكبها، وإمَّا صبرٌ على الطاعة حتَّى يؤدّيها، وإمَّا صبرٌ على البليّة فلا يشكو ربَّه فيها.»

Ibn al-Qayyim, Ṭarīq al-Hijratayn wa Bāb as-Saʿādatayn, ed. al-Iṣlāḥī, 2/577

Translation (ours)Patience is of three kinds: patience away from sin so that he does not commit it, patience upon obedience until he carries it out, and patience through trials so that he does not complain about his Lord in them.

صبرٌ عن المعصية

Away from sin. Holding back when the nafs pushes. This page is about this one.

صبرٌ على الطاعة

Upon obedience. Staying consistent with salah, Quran and duties when it is hard.

صبرٌ على البليّة

Through trials. Holding steady in hardship without complaining about Allah.

Every sin is a bad habit. Not every bad habit is a sin.

A sin

Wrecks your day like any bad habit, and it is disobedience to the One whose help you need to reach anything.

A bad habit

Scrolling till 2am may not be haram, but it still steals your Fajr, your focus and your goals.

So staying away from sins is not a separate project from your goals. It removes the worst habits you have, and it removes what blocks Allah’s help. Ibn al-Qayyim says sins remove blessings (wall 3), and Allah says:

Qur'an

إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يُغَيِّرُ مَا بِقَوْمٍ حَتَّىٰ يُغَيِّرُوا۟ مَا بِأَنفُسِهِمْ

Qur'an, ar-Raʿd 13:11

Sahih International“Indeed, Allah will not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves.”

Leave the sin for Allah, and you clear the road for His help with everything else you are working toward, in shā’ Allāh.

The ten walls

01

See how ugly it really is

العلم بقبحها ورذالتها

«علمُ العبد بقبحها ورذالتها ودناءَتها، وأنّ اللَّه إنّما حرَّمها ونهى عنها صيانةً لعبده وحمايةً عن الدنايا والرذائل، كما يحمي الوالدُ الشفيقُ ولدَه عما يضرّه. وهذا السبب يحمل العاقلَ على تركها، ولو لم يعلَّق عليها وعيدٌ بالعذاب.»

Ibn al-Qayyim, Ṭarīq al-Hijratayn wa Bāb as-Saʿādatayn, ed. al-Iṣlāḥī, 2/588

Translation (ours)The first: the servant knowing how ugly, vile and low the sin is, and that Allah only forbade it to guard His servant and protect him from what is low and degrading, the way a caring father protects his child from what harms him. This reason alone is enough to make a sensible person leave it, even if no punishment had been attached to it.

A word from the Salaf

عَنِ الْأَوْزَاعِيِّ قَالَ: قَالَ بِلَالُ بْنُ سَعْدٍ رَحِمَهُ اللَّهُ: لَا تَنْظُرْ إِلَى صِغَرِ الْخَطِيئَةِ، وَلَكِنِ انْظُرْ إِلَى مَنْ عَصَيْتَ

Abū Nuʿaym, Ḥilyat al-Awliyāʾ 5/223

TranslationAl-Awzāʿī reported that Bilāl ibn Saʿd said: “Do not look at how small the sin is. Look at the One you are disobeying.”

The drill

Write ten honest lines about how ugly this sin really is. Not how it feels in the moment: what it is, what it does, what it has already cost you.

      03

      Protect what He gave you

      مراعاة نعمه وإحسانه

      «مراعاة نعمه عليك وإحسانه إليك، فإنّ الذنوب تزيل النعمَ ولا بدّ. فما أذنبَ عبدٌ ذنبًا إلّا زالت عنه نعمةٌ من اللَّه بحسب ذلك الذنب. فإن تاب وراجع رجعت إليه أو مثلُها، وإن أصرّ لم ترجع إليه.»

      Ibn al-Qayyim, Ṭarīq al-Hijratayn wa Bāb as-Saʿādatayn, ed. al-Iṣlāḥī, 2/588

      Translation (ours)The third: guarding His blessings upon you and His goodness to you, for sins remove blessings, without doubt. No servant commits a sin except that a blessing from Allah leaves him in proportion to that sin. If he repents and returns, it comes back to him or something like it. If he persists, it does not return.

      «فإنّ المعاصي نارُ النعم تأكلها، كما تأكل النارُ الحطبَ»

      Ibn al-Qayyim, Ṭarīq al-Hijratayn wa Bāb as-Saʿādatayn, ed. al-Iṣlāḥī, 2/589

      Translation (ours)“Sins are the fire of blessings: they eat them the way fire eats firewood.”

      Qur'an

      يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ ٱذْكُرُوا۟ نِعْمَتَ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ

      Qur'an, Fāṭir 35:3

      Sahih International“O mankind, remember the favor of Allah upon you.”

      Qur'an

      إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يُغَيِّرُ مَا بِقَوْمٍ حَتَّىٰ يُغَيِّرُوا۟ مَا بِأَنفُسِهِمْ

      Qur'an, ar-Raʿd 13:11

      Sahih International“Indeed, Allah will not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves.”

      The drill

      Write fifteen blessings Allah has given you. Be specific: not “family”, but the name of the person. Then answer the last question honestly.

        04

        Fear Allah and His punishment

        خوف الله وخشية عقابه

        «خوف اللَّه وخشية عقابه. وهذا إنّما يثبت بتصديقه في وعده ووعيده، والإيمان به وبكتابه وبرسوله. وهذا السبب يقوى بالعلم واليقين، ويضعف بضعفهما.»

        Ibn al-Qayyim, Ṭarīq al-Hijratayn wa Bāb as-Saʿādatayn, ed. al-Iṣlāḥī, 2/589

        Translation (ours)The fourth: fear of Allah and dread of His punishment. This only becomes firm by believing Him in His promise and His threat, and by believing in Him, His Book and His Messenger. It grows stronger with knowledge and certainty, and weaker as they weaken.

        When you are walking toward the sin, the nafs whispers: “Allah is the Most Generous, the Most Forgiving.” True. But He described Himself in one breath as forgiving and severe:

        Qur'an

        غَافِرِ ٱلذَّنۢبِ وَقَابِلِ ٱلتَّوْبِ شَدِيدِ ٱلْعِقَابِ

        Qur'an, Ghāfir 40:3

        Sahih International“The forgiver of sin, acceptor of repentance, severe in punishment.”

        Qur'an

        إِنَّمَا يَخْشَى ٱللَّهَ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ ٱلْعُلَمَٰٓؤُا۟

        Qur'an, Fāṭir 35:28

        Sahih International“Only those fear Allah, from among His servants, who have knowledge.”

        The word here is khashyah (خشية): fear that comes from knowing who Allah is. The ʿulamāʾ meant here are not only people with many books. Ibn Kathīr explains:

        Tafsir

        إنما يخشاه حق خشيته العلماء العارفون به؛ لأنه كلما كانت المعرفة للعظيم القدير العليم الموصوف بصفات الكمال المنعوت بالأسماء الحسنى، كلما كانت المعرفة به أتم والعلم به أكمل، كانت الخشية له أعظم وأكثر

        Ibn Kathīr, Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm, on Fāṭir 35:28

        Translation (ours)“Only the ʿulamāʾ who truly know Him fear Him as He deserves to be feared, because the more complete one’s knowledge of the Mighty, the All-Powerful, the All-Knowing, described with every attribute of perfection and the Beautiful Names, the greater and deeper one’s khashyah of Him becomes.” In the same place he quotes Saʿīd ibn Jubayr: “Khashyah is what stands between you and disobeying Allah.”

        05

        Love Allah

        محبة الله

        «محبة اللَّه سبحانه، وهي من أقوى الأسباب في الصبر عن مخالفته ومعاصيه. فإنّ المحب لمن يحب مطيع، وكلما قوي سلطانُ المحبة في القلب كان اقتضاؤه للطاعة وترك المخالفة أقوى، وإنما تصدر المعصية والمخالفة من ضعف المحبّة وسلطانها.»

        Ibn al-Qayyim, Ṭarīq al-Hijratayn wa Bāb as-Saʿādatayn, ed. al-Iṣlāḥī, 2/589

        Translation (ours)The fifth: love of Allah, the Glorified. It is among the strongest reasons for patience against opposing and disobeying Him, for the lover obeys the one he loves. The stronger the rule of love in the heart, the stronger its pull toward obedience and away from disobedience. Disobedience only comes from weakness in that love and its rule.

        «وفَرْقٌ بين من يحمله على ترك معصية سيّده خوفُه من سوطه وعقوبته، وبين من يحمله على ذلك حبُّه لسيّده.»

        Ibn al-Qayyim, Ṭarīq al-Hijratayn wa Bāb as-Saʿādatayn, ed. al-Iṣlāḥī, 2/590

        Translation (ours)“There is a difference between one who leaves disobeying his master out of fear of his whip, and one who leaves it out of love for his master.”

        Think about how each one works. Fear is strongest when you feel watched. Hope comes and goes. But love works whether anyone is there or not: you leave things for someone you love even when they would never find out. That is why love is the wall that holds when the room is empty.

        Translation (ours)Seeing a mother clutch her baby, the Prophet ﷺ asked whether she would ever throw her child into a fire. Then he said: “Allah is more merciful to His servants than she is to her child.”

        Read these out loud, slowly. Then finish the “Didn’t He…?” lines with your own life.

        Nobody waits for me to come back the way Allah does.

          06

          Hold yourself to a higher standard

          شرف النفس وزكاؤها

          «شرفُ النفس وزكاؤها وفضلُها وأنَفتهُا وحميّتُها أن تختار الأسباب التي تحطّها وتضع قدرها، وتخفض منزلتها وتُحقَرها، وتسوّي بينها وبين السَّفِلة.»

          Ibn al-Qayyim, Ṭarīq al-Hijratayn wa Bāb as-Saʿādatayn, ed. al-Iṣlāḥī, 2/590

          Translation (ours)The sixth: the honour of the soul, its purity, its worth, its dignity and its self-respect, refusing to choose the things that drag it down, lower its worth, reduce its rank, make it contemptible, and put it on the same level as the lowest of people.

            08

            Remember how short this is

            قصر الأمل

            «قِصَر الأمل، وعلمُه بسرعة انتقاله، وأنَّه كمسافر دخلَ قريةَ وهو مُزمِعٌ على الخروج منها، أو كراكب قال في ظل شجرة ثمَّ سار وتركها، فهو لعلمه بقلَّة مقامه وسرعة انتقاله حريص على ترك ما يُثقِلُه حملُه ويضرّه ولا ينفعه، حريص على الانتقال بخير ما بحضرته. فليس للقلب أنفعُ من قِصَر الأمل، ولا أضرُّ من التسويف وطول الأمل.»

            Ibn al-Qayyim, Ṭarīq al-Hijratayn wa Bāb as-Saʿādatayn, ed. al-Iṣlāḥī, 2/596-597

            Translation (ours)The eighth: short hopes, and knowing how quickly he will move on: that he is like a traveller who entered a town already set on leaving it, or a rider who rested in the shade of a tree, then moved on and left it. Knowing how short his stay is, he is keen to drop whatever is heavy to carry, harms him and does not benefit him, and keen to leave with the best of what he has. Nothing benefits the heart more than short hopes, and nothing harms it more than procrastination and long hopes.

            Hadith

            كُنْ فِي الدُّنْيَا كَأَنَّكَ غَرِيبٌ، أَوْ عَابِرُ سَبِيلٍ. وَكَانَ ابْنُ عُمَرَ يَقُولُ إِذَا أَمْسَيْتَ فَلاَ تَنْتَظِرِ الصَّبَاحَ، وَإِذَا أَصْبَحْتَ فَلاَ تَنْتَظِرِ الْمَسَاءَ، وَخُذْ مِنْ صِحَّتِكَ لِمَرَضِكَ، وَمِنْ حَيَاتِكَ لِمَوْتِكَ

            Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6416

            Translation (ours)The Prophet ﷺ took hold of Ibn ʿUmar’s shoulder and said: “Be in this world as though you were a stranger or a traveller.” And Ibn ʿUmar used to say: “When evening comes, do not wait for the morning, and when morning comes, do not wait for the evening. Take from your health for your sickness, and from your life for your death.”

            Cut the excess, guard your company

            مجانبة الفضول

            «مجانبة الفضول في مطعمه ومشربه وملبسه ومنامه واجتماعه بالناس. فإنَّ قوَّة الداعي إلى المعاصي إنَّما تنشأ من هذه الفضلات، فإنَّها تطلب لها مصرفًا، فيضيق عليها المباحُ، فتتعدَّاه إلى الحرام، ومن أعظم الأشياء ضررًا على العبد بطالتُه وفراغه، فإنَّ النَّفس لا تقعد فارغة، بل إن لم يشغلها بما ينفعها شَغَلَتْه بما يضرّه ولابدّ.»

            Ibn al-Qayyim, Ṭarīq al-Hijratayn wa Bāb as-Saʿādatayn, ed. al-Iṣlāḥī, 2/597

            Translation (ours)The ninth: avoiding excess in food, drink, clothing, sleep and mixing with people. The strength of the pull toward sin grows out of these excesses: they demand an outlet, the permissible becomes too narrow for them, and they spill over into the forbidden. Among the most harmful things for a servant are idleness and free time, because the soul never sits empty: if he does not occupy it with what benefits him, it will occupy him with what harms him, without doubt.

            Qur'an

            وَٱصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ مَعَ ٱلَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم بِٱلْغَدَوٰةِ وَٱلْعَشِىِّ يُرِيدُونَ وَجْهَهُۥ

            Qur'an, al-Kahf 18:28

            Sahih International“And keep yourself patient [by being] with those who call upon their Lord in the morning and the evening, seeking His countenance.”

            Strengthen the tree of iman

            ثبات شجرة الإيمان في القلب

            «وهو الجامع لهذه الأسباب كلّها، وهو: ثبات شجرة الإيمان في القلب. فصبر العبد عن المعاصي إنَّما هو بحسب قوَّة إيمانه، فكلَّما كان إيمانه أقوى كان صبره أتمّ، وإذا ضعف الإيمان ضعف الصبر.»

            Ibn al-Qayyim, Ṭarīq al-Hijratayn wa Bāb as-Saʿādatayn, ed. al-Iṣlāḥī, 2/597

            Translation (ours)The tenth, which gathers all of these reasons: the firmness of the tree of iman in the heart. A servant’s patience against sins is only in proportion to the strength of his iman: the stronger his iman, the more complete his patience, and when iman weakens, patience weakens.

            «ومن ظنَّ أنَّه يقوى على ترك المخالفات والمعاصي بدون الإيمان الرَّاسخ الثابت، فقد غلط.»

            Ibn al-Qayyim, Ṭarīq al-Hijratayn wa Bāb as-Saʿādatayn, ed. al-Iṣlāḥī, 2/597

            Translation (ours)“Whoever thinks he can manage to leave sins and disobedience without firm, deep-rooted iman is mistaken.”
